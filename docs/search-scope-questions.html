Questions for the client

How should the search actually work?

The scanner runs and finds real prices. Before we widen it, we need decisions on what it should search, how often, and what counts as a good enough price. Twelve questions, each with our recommendation.

The core choice

There are two ways to look for a cheap long-term rental, and they answer different questions. The system is built for the first. The second is possible and would take about a day to add.

A · Scheduled sweep

Built and working today

  • Runs itself every morning at 06:00
  • Searches a fixed list of locations we set in advance
  • Answers “where in the country is something unusually cheap?”
  • Spots price drops because it sees the same deals every day
  • Costs money every single day, whether or not you look

B · On-demand search

Possible, roughly a day of work

  • You name a place, a date and a length
  • Searches only that, results in about a minute
  • Answers “what can I get in Denver from 2 November?”
  • No price history, because it only looks once
  • Costs only when someone asks

They are not mutually exclusive

The most useful setup is usually a small daily sweep over a handful of locations, giving you price history where it matters, plus the ability to ask a one-off question about anywhere else. That is also the cheapest way to keep wide coverage available without paying for it daily.

Decide these first

These five block everything else. The rest of the project is shaped by the answers.

Question 01

Which way should the scanner work?

  • Scheduled sweep only. Daily report over a fixed location list.
  • On-demand only. Nothing runs unless someone asks.
  • Both. A small daily sweep, plus one-off questions.
Our suggestion Both, but build on-demand first. It costs almost nothing to run, answers real questions immediately, and it tells us which locations are worth putting into the daily sweep instead of guessing.
Question 02

How many locations, and how many pickup dates?

This is the single biggest cost driver. Each location and each pickup date multiplies the daily bill.

  • 5 locations, 1 pickup date — about $2.35 a day, roughly $70 a month
  • 10 locations, 1 pickup date — about $4.70 a day, roughly $141 a month
  • 10 locations, 3 pickup dates — about $14 a day, roughly $423 a month
  • 250 locations, 3 pickup dates — about $352 a day, roughly $10,575 a month
Our suggestion Start with 5 to 10 airports and a single pickup date. Nationwide daily coverage at full depth is not affordable on this data source, and the original estimate of $35 to $60 a month does not survive contact with the real pricing.
Question 03

Is $1,100 per month still the target?

At Atlanta, nothing met it. The cheapest large SUV was $1,178, the cheapest minivan $1,493, and the cheapest 12-passenger van $1,651. The only options below $1,100 were peer-to-peer listings, which are excluded and cannot be extended into a monthly programme.

  • Keep $1,100 and accept that most days report nothing
  • Raise it to a figure the market actually offers
  • Set a different target for each vehicle type
Our suggestion Set a separate target per vehicle type, chosen after a week of real scanning. Atlanta is one city and may simply be expensive; a week of data across several cities would tell us what “cheap” really looks like.
Question 04

Is a 30-day booking an acceptable stand-in for the 330-day rental?

No booking engine will quote a single 330-day rental. Long-term hire is sold as monthly programmes instead, such as Enterprise Month-or-More from 28 days or Hertz Multi-Month from 63. The scanner therefore prices a 30-day rental and notes which programme each supplier offers.

This question was raised at the planning stage and has never been answered. Everything built so far assumes the answer is yes.

Our suggestion Confirm explicitly, because if the answer is no, the approach needs rethinking before any more is spent.
Question 05

Who confirms with a supplier that the extension actually works?

Nobody has yet checked with Enterprise or Avis that a 30-day booking really can be extended toward eleven months, or what it costs when you do. This is a phone call, not a piece of software, and the whole premise rests on it.

Our suggestion Someone should call one branch this week and ask about one specific vehicle. Until then, every monthly figure the system reports is a 30-day price, not a proven long-term rate.

Then these, to shape the searches

Smaller decisions. Each has a working default, so none of them blocks progress.

Question 06

Do you ever need one-way rentals?

Today every search returns the vehicle to the same place, as the brief asked. The data source does support a different drop-off city, so this can be added.

Default if unanswered Same-location only.
Question 07

What pickup time should we use?

Currently 10:00 for every search. Time affects both price and availability. The data source only accepts whole hours, and offers nothing between 01:00 and 05:00.

Default if unanswered 10:00, pickup and return.
Question 08

Should we price other rental lengths?

Only 30 days today. Some programmes need longer commitments to unlock, notably Avis Flex at 60 days and Hertz Multi-Month at 63, so a 60-day search might reveal rates a 30-day search never shows.

Our suggestion Add a 60-day search for a week and compare. It doubles that portion of the cost.
Question 09

What is the driver's age?

Set to 30. Drivers under 25 or over 70 attract counter surcharges that these quotes do not include, so the reported price would understate what is actually paid.

Default if unanswered 30.
Question 10

Should peer-to-peer listings stay excluded?

Turo is currently filtered out, because the brief excluded it and it has no monthly programme. It was also consistently the cheapest option at Atlanta, sometimes several hundred dollars below the rental companies.

Our suggestion Keep it excluded, but say so knowingly: it is the only thing that came in under budget.
Question 11

Where should the daily report go?

Reports are written to disk and shown on the dashboard. Email and Slack delivery are built but switched off, because they need an address or a webhook.

Needed from you An email address, a Slack webhook, or confirmation that the dashboard is enough.
Question 12

Who runs the on-demand searches, and how?

If you want on-demand search, it can be a command a developer runs, or a search box on the dashboard. The search box is friendlier but means the website can spend money, so it would need a daily limit to stop an accidental run of queries draining the account.

Our suggestion Start with the command, add the search box once the daily budget is agreed.
Prepared 7 September 2026. All Atlanta prices and all costs quoted here were measured from live scans on that date. Prices are quotes captured at a moment in time, not reservations, and must be confirmed with the supplier before booking.
